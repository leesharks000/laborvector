              Current Update
              September 15, 2026


              The Budget Lab's labor market analysis, updated to incorporate August 2026 CPS
              microdata, does not provide clear evidence of labor market disruption associated with
              AI. Churn across occupations, AI exposure among the unemployed, and usage data all
              remain flat, lie within historical ranges, or continue along pre-AI trends.

              Of course, these findings may change as new data become available. The Budget Lab
              plans to continue monitoring AI-relevant trends to assess how job impacts might
              change.




              Introduction
              This interactive tool contains The Budget Lab's ongoing analysis of potential AI
              impacts on the labor market. Major categories of analysis can be accessed by clicking
              tabs towards the top of the page with the following names: Occupational churn, AI
              metrics, and Comparisons of AI-exposed and unexposed workers. After selecting one
              of those tabs, users should note the options listed on the left-hand sidebar. Those
              options generate different views of the data: for example, occupational churn (i.e.,
              dissimilarity) can be tracked for the entire labor force or for specific industries.

              Detailed descriptions of the analysis can be found in the Budget Lab documents that
              introduce each. Occupational churn, AI-exposure rates of the unemployed, and related
