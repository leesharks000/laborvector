 Worker Info Exchange partners with TUC on “landmark” new reportexposing the human cost of Uber’s dynamic pay

 April 29, 2026

 Powerful, first-hand driver testimonies reveal true cost of government inaction on enforcing
Supreme Court judgement on worker status 

 We need enforcement rather than reform. Drivers are already workers, but government has no
appetite for enforcing the law.

 Worker Info Exchange (WIE), a non‑profit organisation turning worker data into collective power ,
has today welcomed the TUC’s new report, The Human Price of Dynamic Pay , as a landmark
investigation into the impact of algorithmic pay systems on drivers’ lives.

 WIE was research partner with the TUC on the project to document how Uber’s move to
dynamic pay has deepened insecurity across the ride‑hail and delivery economy.

 The report draws on powerful first-hand testimony from eleven Uber and delivery drivers who
describe how the company’s “dynamic pay” system, a constantly shifting, algorithmic model that
determines how much workers earn on each job, has eroded income stability, increased unpaid
hours, and worsened mental and physical health.

 WIE, through its Dynamic Pay campaign , is currently coordinating a collective legal action
against Uber in the Dutch courts, arguing that the company’s dynamic pricing systems breach
drivers’ data protection and transparency rights under EU law.

 James Farrar, Director of WIE, comments: “Today’s report lays bare what thousands of
gig‑economy workers already know from daily experience. That dynamic pay is not innovation,
it’s exploitation by algorithm, a business model built on secrecy and unequal power. And it isn’t
just a drivers’ issue. It’s a warning of where all work could go for all of us if we don’t act.

 Dynamic pay undermines every foundation of fair bargaining. You can’t negotiate a wage when
an algorithm decides a different price for everyone. Ending this system and restoring
transparency are the first steps toward justice for all platform workers.”

 Cansu Safak, Research Lead at WIE, comments: “This report documents the severe human
cost of Uber’s dynamic pay: a decline in wages that our earlier participatory audit with Oxford
University, built from drivers’ own data subject access requests, had already exposed in
numbers. These testimonies add the lived reality behind that data.

 The absence of basic worker rights has allowed Uber’s dynamic pay to thrive. While the EU and
many other jurisdictions are strengthening protections for platform workers, the UK has been
slow to take similar measures. This permissiveness has emboldened Uber to not only to violate
employment law but also breach data protection law, using opaque algorithmic wage-setting to
deepen exploitation.With no transparency over the conditions they work under, drivers have
been forced to turn to data protection law as the only remaining route to assert their rights. And
in the absence of meaningful regulatory response, they are once again turning to the courts to
seek justice through the collective legal action we have launched.”

 WIE’s Dynamic Pay legal action

 WIE’s Dynamic Pay campaign is coordinating a collective legal claim against Uber in the Dutch
courts, challenging the company’s algorithmic wage‑setting under EU data protection and
transparency laws. The case seeks to expose the hidden mechanisms that shape driver pay
and work allocation across Europe.

 WIE argues that Uber’s use of algorithmically set dynamic pay is unlawful under Article 22 of
the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), which gives drivers the right not to be
subject to decisions based solely on automated decision-making, including profiling. Uber is
also not transparent about how its algorithm works, and has used drivers’ own historical data to
train the system that now determines their pay. WIE believes this is not only unfair but unlawful,
and that drivers are entitled to compensation.

 Separately, Uber has already been fined for unlawfully transferring driver data to the United
States between August 2021 and September 2023. WIE believes drivers are also entitled to
compensation for this breach.

 The claim pushes for greater transparency, legal accountability, and remedies for drivers whose
pay has been reduced or made volatile by Uber’s system, and seeks to expose the hidden
mechanisms that shape driver pay and work allocation across Europe.

 Joint analysis by Oxford University and WIE , covering 1.5 million Uber trips
between 2016 and 2024, found that after the introduction of dynamic pricing in 2023, drivers’ real
pay declined, volatility increased, and Uber’s profit margins rose sharply.

 Under the system, two drivers performing identical trips can be paid vastly different amounts,
with Uber keeping more than half of the fare in many cases.

 About Worker Info Exchange

 Worker Info Exchange (WIE) is an independent non‑profit organisation that helps platform
workers turn their data into power. Founded in 2020, WIE works with drivers, couriers and
trade‑union partners to:

 Secure workers’ right to access and understand the data that controls their work.

 Investigate how algorithms determine pay, performance and job allocation.

 Build data trusts so workers can combine and analyse their data collectively.

 Support unions and grassroots organisations in using data for collective action,
bargaining and transparency.

 Through this work, WIE is pioneering a worker‑centred approach to digital rights — ensuring
that technology serves people, not the other way round.
