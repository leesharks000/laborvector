Tracking the Impact of AI on the Labor Market

Gains and lossesinteractive toolactive
Tracking the Impact of AI on the Labor Market: an interactive tool (tracker) with regular updates. Operator: The Budget Lab at Yale (Yale University); the first report by Martha Gimbel, Molly Kinder (Senior Fellow, Brookings Metro), Joshua Kendall and Maddie Lee. Its unit: monthly CPS microdata analysed in three sections: occupational churn (dissimilarity), AI metrics (including AI exposure among the unemployed and usage measures), and comparisons of AI-exposed and unexposed workers. Whose labor: the occupational mix of the labor force, employment and unemployment, as they relate to measures of AI exposure and AI usage.

What it findsthe first report (1 October 2025): the broader labor market has not experienced a discernible disruption since ChatGPT's release 33 months earlier; the occupational mix is not yet changing in ways that clearly align with the introduction of AI; measures of AI usage show no connection to changes in employment or unemployment; a synthetic differences-in-differences analysis of AI exposure does not yet clearly indicate an AI-related labor market footprint; September 2026: churn, AI exposure among the unemployed and usage data remain flat, within historical ranges, or on pre-AI trends
Its stated limitsthe findings may change as new data become available; the first report: the analysis is not predictive of the future; the first report: data from Claude usage alone is not representative of how workers across the economy use AI chatbots and tools

What this evidence can settle

Can answer

  • whether occupational churn has departed from historical ranges since AI's introduction
  • whether AI-exposed workers are over-represented among the unemployed
  • whether AI usage measures track changes in employment or unemployment

Cannot answer

  • who supplies, directs or gains from labor performed with AI
  • effects below the level the CPS can detect
  • how workers across the economy use AI, beyond one provider's usage data and a theoretical exposure measure

In the worked questions

  • Which sources observe employment change, and which estimate exposure?
  • Who receives the gains of labor performed with or for AI, and who bears the costs?
