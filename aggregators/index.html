Labor and AI: the meta-aggregator

The resources that observe the intersections of labor and AI, from data workers' own inquiries and contract inventories to employment trackers and records of how people use AI systems, each formalized as an entity. Every record answers one question: who supplies the labor, who directs it, and who receives its gains — and what kind of evidence does this resource bring to that question? 15 resources, compiled 2026-10-29.

Who supplies the labor: annotators and moderators, platform and warehouse workers, writers whose work enters training, the people commissioning a system and correcting it. Each record says whose work its evidence is about.

Who directs it: the employer, the platform, the vendor's software, the model, the user's commission. Where the direction is automated (hiring, scheduling, quotas, discipline) the record says by what.

Who receives its gains: wages and employment, workload, credit and ownership, and who bears the costs. A record states only what its resource measures or reports.

On what evidence: an exposure estimate, an observed employment outcome, a worker's testimony, a negotiated provision, a transcript of interactions, a rating against standards, a policy instrument. Each kind answers a different part of the question; the records keep them apart.

An exposure estimate, an employment outcome, a worker's testimony, a negotiated protection and a record of interactions each show a different part of the same labor question. Tap a step for the fields that answer it.

Five intersections

  • Labor producing AI: annotation, moderation, evaluation, maintenance, and creative or scholarly work entering training and retrieval
  • AI governing labor: hiring, surveillance, scheduling, quotas, evaluation and discipline
  • Labor performed with AI: assistance, delegation, task fidelity, correction burdens and user labor drag
  • Gains and losses: wages, employment, workload, credit, ownership, and who captures productivity gains
  • Worker power: bargaining, organizing, consent, refusal, contestation and enforceable protections

Data Workers' Inquiry: a global, radically participatory research initiative; community-based research project with a public repository of worker-made inquiries. Operator: Distributed AI Research Institute (DAIR) and the Weizenbaum Institute, with TU Berlin; project lead Milagros Miceli. GDPval: a benchmark (an evaluation dataset with an experimental automated grader). Operator: OpenAI: the paper's 19 authors (Tejal Patwardhan first; nine marked equal contribution) give OpenAI as their affiliation, with correspondence to an openai.com address; OpenAI's own post at this address answered 403 and is unread. Worked questions

A research question, the kind of evidence that can answer it, and the resources that hold that evidence.

Where are actual negotiated protections against automated monitoring and discipline?

A protection is evidence when it is written into an agreement or a rule. Contract text and policy instruments hold it; estimates and testimony do not.

  • Negotiating Tech: An Inventory of U.S. Union Contract Provisions for the Digital Age
    U.S. union contract provisions on notice, information, monitoring, data rights and job security, each linked to its agreement
  • Digital Labour Platform Policy Tracker
    laws, policies and EU platform-work collective agreements, including rules on algorithmic management
  • Luddite Lab Resource Hub
    organizing toolkits and examples of pro-worker contract language on automation and AI

None of the three records whether a provision is enforced or what it changes at work.

Which sources observe employment change, and which estimate exposure?

An exposure estimate scores what AI could do to a task; an observed outcome counts what happened to jobs. They answer different questions and are often cited as if they answered the same one.

  • Canaries Dashboard
    observed: employment by age and AI exposure in ADP payroll records, monthly
  • Tracking the Impact of AI on the Labor Market
    observed: occupational churn and unemployment by exposure, from the CPS
  • Generative AI and Jobs: A Refined Global Index of Occupational Exposure
    estimated: the technical feasibility of GenAI automation, task by task, for occupations worldwide
  • Working with AI: Measuring the Applicability of Generative AI to Occupations
    estimated from use: an applicability score built from Bing Copilot conversations
  • Anthropic Economic Index
    observed use on one provider's product: the share of O*NET tasks performed with Claude

No record establishes that exposure causes the employment change; the two observed trackers reach different readings of the same period.

Who does the data work that trains AI, and on what terms?

The labor inside training and evaluation is recorded by the workers themselves, by ratings against labor standards, and by guidance to the companies that buy it. Each holds a different part.

  • Data Workers' Inquiry
    inquiries written by data workers on their own workplaces, pay and organizing
  • Fairwork Cloudwork Ratings
    platforms rated against five fair-work principles, with a survey of 776 cloudworkers
  • Turkopticon
    workers' ratings of Mechanical Turk requesters on pay, fairness and communication
  • Responsible Sourcing Across the Data Supply Line
    guidance to AI practitioners on paying and sourcing data enrichment workers

None gives wages or conditions at the scale of the industry, or how much of a given system's training data any reported work produced.

What does automated management of workers look like, and can a worker see the data it uses?

The products, the rules that govern them and the data a worker can recover are three different records of the same direction of labor.

  • Bossware and Employment Tech Database
    an inventory of workplace technology products, 2020 to 2021, with their stated terms on consent and worker access to data
  • Worker Info Exchange
    data obtained for gig workers from platforms, on how work was allocated, paid and assessed
  • Digital Labour Platform Policy Tracker
    the rules that address algorithmic management on platforms

The product inventory is a 2021 snapshot, and no record measures how often or with what effect a product is used at a workplace.

How do people use AI systems in their own work, and how is that use recorded?

A provider's record of its own users, a classification of one assistant's conversations, and a benchmark of constructed tasks each describe labor performed with AI from a different position.

  • Anthropic Economic Index
    Claude conversations mapped to tasks and collaboration modes, with task success as Claude assesses it: one provider's own product
  • Working with AI: Measuring the Applicability of Generative AI to Occupations
    Bing Copilot conversations, 2024, classified as user goals and AI actions
  • GDPval
    frontier models' deliverables on tasks built from professionals' work, graded against experts'

None records, from the commissioning side, whether the system's labor advanced the task the user set, or who receives the time it saves. That is the question DSL measures.

Who receives the gains of labor performed with or for AI, and who bears the costs?

The census's organizing question in its sharpest form. Its records hold costs more often than gains.

  • Canaries Dashboard
    costs as observed: employment of young workers in exposed occupations against its counterfactual
  • Fairwork Cloudwork Ratings
    pay and conditions on the platforms rated, against minimum standards
  • Anthropic Economic Index
    estimated time savings by task, without who captures them
  • Tracking the Impact of AI on the Labor Market
    no clear labor-market footprint yet, at the level the CPS can see

No record in the census measures the distribution of productivity gains between workers, employers, platforms and model providers.
