Anthropic Economic Index

Labor performed with AIGains and lossesreport seriesactive
Anthropic Economic Index: a report series with data releases: six reports and their datasets, from the operator's own product usage. Operator: Anthropic (its Economics team). Its unit: a sampled Claude conversation (Claude.ai consumer chat and Cowork) or a 1P API prompt-response record, classified by Claude to an O*NET task and to economic primitives; since June 2026 also a survey respondent linked to up to 20 of their own sessions. Whose labor: the tasks people bring to Anthropic's own product Claude, mapped to the O*NET tasks of the occupations that typically perform them; observed on Claude.ai, Cowork, Claude Code and Anthropic's first-party API only.

The Anthropic Economic Index provides insights into how AI is being incorporated into real-world tasks across the modern economy.

In its own words: https://huggingface.co/datasets/Anthropic/EconomicIndex/raw/main/README.md, read 2026-10-29

Who supplies, who directs, who gains

Supplies the laborClaude users and 1P API customers bring the tasks; Claude performs them, recorded as the share of occupational tasks 'we already see being done with Claude'
Directs itthe user, recorded as collaboration mode (directive, feedback loop, task iteration, validation, learning) and as the autonomy users delegate to Claude on a 1-5 scale; users choose which tasks to bring
Receives the gains, bears the costsnot recorded as a distribution; the reports estimate aggregate US labor productivity effects and record survey respondents' self-reported productivity gains and their expectations about pay, job security and shared gains

Each as the resource itself records it. Where it records nothing, the census says so.

What it records

IntersectionsLabor performed with AI: assistance, delegation, task fidelity, correction burdens and user labor drag; Distribution of gains and losses: wages, employment, workload, credit, ownership, and who captures productivity gains
Kind of evidenceinteraction dataexposure estimateworker testimonydata access
Unit of recorda sampled Claude conversation (Claude.ai consumer chat and Cowork) or a 1P API prompt-response record, classified by Claude to an O*NET task and to economic primitives; since June 2026 also a survey respondent linked to up to 20 of their own sessions
Whose laborthe tasks people bring to Anthropic's own product Claude, mapped to the O*NET tasks of the occupations that typically perform them; observed on Claude.ai, Cowork, Claude Code and Anthropic's first-party API only
Methodprivacy-preserving classification of sampled transcripts by Claude (another instance of Claude reads the transcripts); task mapping to O*NET; classifiers for five economic primitives validated for directional accuracy; since June 2026 continuous daily sampling and a survey of Claude users linked to their usage op
Coveragegeography: global Claude usage, with country and US-state breakdowns from IP geolocation; period: reports from February 2025 to June 2026; the January 2026 report samples 13-20 November 2025; the June 2026 report samples 10 April to 10 June 2026
What it findsaugmentation 52% and automation 45% of Claude.ai conversations in November 2025; 1P API traffic three-quarters automation; top ten tasks 24% of Claude.ai conversations and 32% of 1P API traffic; the most common task is modifying software to correct errors; 49% of jobs have seen AI usage for at least a quarter of their tasks (across reports); removing the tasks Claude covers produces a net deskilling effect across most occupations; implied labor productivity growth of 1.8 percentage points a year falls to about 1.0-1.2 once task success is factored in; survey: 10% of respondents rate losing their own job as likely or very likely; those who use Claude in the most automated way are the most optimistic about pay and job security
Its stated limitstask success is Claude's own assessment; primitives are directionally accurate and not exact; observed success rates reflect users' selection of tasks; the survey is not representative of the general population; the primitives are not shown to be causal; the implications depend on how often conversations displace or augment work

What this evidence can settle

Can answer

  • which O*NET tasks are performed with Claude, and in what share
  • how users collaborate with Claude (automation and augmentation modes, delegated autonomy) by task, occupation, country and product surface
  • how Claude-assessed task success and estimated time savings vary with task complexity
  • what Claude users report and expect about their own work, linked to their usage

Cannot answer

  • AI use on other providers' systems
  • observed employment or wage change
  • whether a conversation displaces or augments work that would otherwise be done by a person
  • who captures the productivity gains it estimates
  • task success as judged by the user or by an external grader

In the worked questions

  • Which sources observe employment change, and which estimate exposure?
  • How do people use AI systems in their own work, and how is that use recorded?
  • Who receives the gains of labor performed with or for AI, and who bears the costs?

Where it meets Labor Vector

rd The archive's reading: the collaboration modes and the AI-autonomy and task-success primitives classify who directs a Claude task and whether Claude completed it, as Claude assesses its own completion; this is the nearest of the resource's measures to DSL's question of whether the system's labor advanced the commissioned task. Its turn counts (users engage more, 1.53 times as many turns, in higher-wage occupations) and task iteration mode bear on user labor drag as the resource records them.

  • NOT an employment outcome series: it observes usage and survey responses

  • the Economic Index Survey is NOT representative of the general population
