Responsible Sourcing Across the Data Supply Line

Labor producing AIGains and lossesworkstream and resource libraryactive
Responsible Sourcing Across the Data Supply Line: a workstream and resource library: recommendations and practitioner tools (guidelines, checklist, templates, a case study, a white paper). Operator: Partnership on AI (PAI), AI, Labor, and the Economy programme; the guidelines developed in partnership with DeepMind. Its unit: a guideline or practical tool addressed to AI practitioners (five guidelines; six resources in the library). Whose labor: data enrichment work: data preparation and cleaning (annotation, intent recognition, sentiment analysis, image recognition, speech-to-text validation) and human-review work (content moderation, feedback loops, validating algorithmic outputs and models).

the Responsible Sourcing workstream aims to develop recommendations and actionable resources to improve the working conditions of these professionals.

In its own words: https://partnershiponai.org/workstream/responsible-sourcing/, read 2026-10-29

Who supplies, who directs, who gains

Supplies the labordata enrichment workers, described as faraway workers under precarious conditions
Directs itAI practitioners (data scientists, AI engineers, product managers) who design data enrichment projects and set payment terms, and the vendors and platforms they source through
Receives the gains, bears the costsnot recorded as a quantity; the sources state that the devaluing of this work brings poor working conditions for workers and worse outcomes for AI development

Each as the resource itself records it. Where it records nothing, the census says so.

IntersectionsLabor producing AI: annotation, moderation, evaluation, maintenance, and creative or scholarly work entering training and retrieval; Distribution of gains and losses: wages, employment, workload, credit, ownership, and who captures productivity gains
Kind of evidencepractitioner guidance
Unit of recorda guideline or practical tool addressed to AI practitioners (five guidelines; six resources in the library)
Whose labordata enrichment work: data preparation and cleaning (annotation, intent recognition, sentiment analysis, image recognition, speech-to-text validation) and human-review work (content moderation, feedback loops, validating algorithmic outputs and models) op
Methodguidance drafted from PAI's white paper, developed with DeepMind and tested and adopted by DeepMind organization-wide; feedback invited from practitioners, workers and the AI community
Coveragegeography: data enrichment workers around the world (not otherwise specified); period: workstream posts from December 2020 to April 2026; library and guidelines published November 2022
What it findsthe sources hold recommendations and report no measured findings of their own; the library states that research shows data enrichment workers often face inconsistent and inappropriate compensation, unclear instructions, lack of recognition and stress from long, ad-hoc hours; guideline 1: pay all data enrichment workers above the local living wage, including time spent reading instructions and in training
Its stated limitsthe guidelines were tested at one AI developer (DeepMind); the library asks for feedback on how they can be improved; PAI cautions against setting per-task rates from a simple median completion time

What this evidence can settle

Can answer

  • what PAI recommends AI practitioners do when sourcing enriched data (pay, pilots, worker selection, instructions, communication)
  • what tools PAI provides to compare vendors and look up local living wages

Cannot answer

  • what data enrichment workers are actually paid
  • which AI developers follow the guidelines, beyond DeepMind
  • whether the guidelines changed conditions for workers (the DeepMind case study was not read)

In the worked questions

  • Who does the data work that trains AI, and on what terms?

Where it meets Labor Vector

rd none stated. The archive reads guideline 1's instruction to count time spent reading instructions, training and reviewing work before submission as the resource's own accounting of uncompensated labor around a task; it stays in PAI's terms.
