Digital Labour Platform Policy Tracker

Worker powerAI governing laborglobal policy trackeractive
Digital Labour Platform Policy Tracker: a global policy tracker (a database of legislation, policies and decisions, with a collective bargaining agreements component and a third category, Other initiatives). Operator: International Labour Organization (ILO), within its Observatory on AI and Work in the Digital Economy. Its unit: a national or regional legislative or policy initiative on platform work; in the CBA component, a collective bargaining agreement between trade unions and platform companies; in Other initiatives, a regional or local initiative (guidelines, protocols). Whose labor: work and employment on digital labour platforms.

Who supplies, who directs, who gains

Supplies the laborworkers on digital labour platforms
Directs itplatform companies, with the automated decision-making systems (algorithms) the tracked rules address; governments regulate and trade unions bargain
Receives the gains, bears the costsnot recorded as outcomes; the tracked rules address remuneration, social security, occupational safety and health, and contract termination

Each as the resource itself records it. Where it records nothing, the census says so.

What it records

IntersectionsWorker power: bargaining, organizing, consent, refusal, contestation and enforceable protections; AI governing labor: hiring, surveillance, scheduling, quotas, evaluation and discipline; Distribution of gains and losses: wages, employment, workload, credit, ownership, and who captures productivity gains
Kind of evidencepolicy trackernegotiated provision
Unit of recorda national or regional legislative or policy initiative on platform work; in the CBA component, a collective bargaining agreement between trade unions and platform companies; in Other initiatives, a regional or local initiative (guidelines, protocols) op
Whose laborwork and employment on digital labour platforms
Methodcompilation of national and regional laws, policies and decisions, and of collective bargaining agreements, described by content, scope and legal status
Coveragegeography: global for legislation: the home page grid lists 33 countries; collective bargaining agreements 'publicly available in Europe' (the home page; the CBA expansion item says within the European Union, and the grid lists a Swiss agreement); period: launched 14 August 2025; CBA component added 7 November 2025
What it findsnone stated as findings: the tracker is presented as an overview of regulations for governments, employers' and workers' representatives
Its stated limitscoverage is still being expanded: the next phase plans wider geographic and thematic coverage and the integration of judicial decisions; the tracker currently focuses on legislative developments worldwide and includes collective bargaining agreements publicly available in Europe; agreements in other regions and judicial decisions are to be developed

What this evidence can settle

Can answer

  • which jurisdictions have legislative or policy initiatives on platform work, and on which issues (employment status, remuneration, algorithms, working time, data, safety, termination, collective bargaining)
  • which publicly available platform-work collective agreements exist in Europe, at what level, and what they cover
  • which regional and local initiatives (guidelines, protocols) on platform work it lists

Cannot answer

  • how the rules are enforced or what they change for platform workers
  • judicial decisions, before the planned next phase
  • what the individual records hold, which this row has not opened (it has read the home page only)

In the worked questions

  • Where are actual negotiated protections against automated monitoring and discipline?
  • What does automated management of workers look like, and can a worker see the data it uses?

Where it meets Labor Vector

rd The tracker records the legal and bargained rules laid over platform work, including rules on automated decision-making systems; it meets the directionality stack at the layer of rules on who may direct labor and how. None stated on DSL, task-origin or user labor drag.
