Data Workers' Inquiry

Labor producing AIWorker powerparticipatory research initiativeactive
Data Workers' Inquiry: a global, radically participatory research initiative; community-based research project with a public repository of worker-made inquiries. Operator: Distributed AI Research Institute (DAIR) and the Weizenbaum Institute, with TU Berlin; project lead Milagros Miceli. Its unit: an inquiry: one data worker's research in their own workplace, published in a format the worker chose (zine, documentary, comic, essay, podcast, animation, report). Whose labor: data labeling, content moderation, data annotation, chatbot impersonation and other data processing that enables AI systems, done by workers at outsourcing companies and platforms (Sama, Cloud Factory, Remotasks, Telus International, Scale AI, Amazon Mechanical Turk, TikTok and Apple's outsourced workforce among those named); invited pieces extend it to artists, translators, writers, nurses and internet users who produce data for AI without compensation.

Who supplies, who directs, who gains

Supplies the labordata workers, often in the Global Majority, precariously employed and politically dispersed, as the project states; in the inquiries, content moderators and annotators in Kenya, Syria, Lebanon, Venezuela, Brazil, Germany, France, Spain and the UK
Directs itoutsourcing companies and platforms, as the inquiries name them (Sama, Cloud Factory, Remotasks, Telus International, Scale AI, Amazon Mechanical Turk), working for client firms named in the inquiries (Meta, TikTok, Apple)
Receives the gains, bears the costsnot recorded as a quantity; the inquiries record the costs workers bear (psychological trauma, low pay, unpaid time, gift-card payment, account shutdowns, layoffs) and name the firms the work serves

What it records

IntersectionsLabor producing AI: annotation, moderation, evaluation, maintenance, and creative or scholarly work entering training and retrieval; Worker power: bargaining, organizing, consent, refusal, contestation and enforceable protections; AI governing labor: hiring, surveillance, scheduling, quotas, evaluation and discipline
Kind of evidenceworker testimonyorganizing resource
Unit of recordan inquiry: one data worker's research in their own workplace, published in a format the worker chose (zine, documentary, comic, essay, podcast, animation, report)
Whose labordata labeling, content moderation, data annotation, chatbot impersonation and other data processing that enables AI systems, done by workers at outsourcing companies and platforms (Sama, Cloud Factory, Remotasks, Telus International, Scale AI, Amazon Mechanical Turk, TikTok and Apple's outsourced workforce among those named); invited pieces extend it to artists, translators, writers, nurses and internet users who produce data for AI without compensation op
Methodworker-written inquiry: participatory action research in which data workers act as community researchers, set their own questions, design and conduct the inquiry (interviews with colleagues among them) and choose the presentation format; the organizing team trains them in data collection and analysis and monitors legal and ethical boundaries
Coveragegeography: nine countries across five continents (the home page); Kenya, Venezuela, Syria, Lebanon and Germany at launch; period: project phase at the Weizenbaum Institute August 2023 to November 2024; the repository continues on data-workers.org (copyright 2026)
What it findsthe repository presents data workers' realities ranging from psychological, physical and sexual abuse to resistance strategies and efforts to unionize; outsourcing companies prey on vulnerable populations, such as refugees, offering them work with little compensation (the Weizenbaum project page); named inquiries document Remotasks shutting down thousands of accounts of African data workers in March 2024, and Amazon Mechanical Turk paying data workers with gift cards
Its stated limitsnone stated as limits; the project states that epistemic authority remains with the workers, so each inquiry speaks for its author and the colleagues the author consulted

What this evidence can settle

Can answer

  • what data workers themselves identify as the urgent issues in their workplaces
  • how named outsourcers and platforms manage, pay and discipline data workers, in the workers' own accounts
  • how data workers organize (the African Content Moderators Union, the Data Labelers Association)

Cannot answer

  • how prevalent any reported condition is across the data-work industry
  • wage or employment figures at scale
  • how much of a given AI system's training data any reported work produced

In the worked questions

  • Who does the data work that trains AI, and on what terms?

Where it meets Labor Vector

rd The inquiries record labor producing AI from the worker's side, including work in which a person performs as the machine ('human chatbots' at Scale AI; 'When users think they are talking to a machine, human emotion becomes data'); the archive reads this as bearing on task-origin, where the attribution of a composition to the system conceals the human labor that produced it.
