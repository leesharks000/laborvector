Worker Info Exchange

AI governing laborWorker powernon-profit organisationactive
Worker Info Exchange: a non-profit organisation: data access requests, data investigations, data trusts and strategic litigation for gig workers. Operator: Worker Info Exchange, a non-profit in London (Director James Farrar; Research Lead Cansu Safak, as the foundation's post of 29 April 2026 names them); the Uber collective action is brought by Stichting WIE International, a Dutch foundation working in cooperation with Worker Info Exchange, and the foundation's post describes WIE as coordinating it. Its unit: a subject access or data portability request made on behalf of a worker; also a targeted data investigation or report. Whose labor: the work of gig economy workers managed by algorithms (Uber drivers in the UK and Europe named in its news; its reports include a Just Eat Report and a McMisery Report).

Worker Info Exchange is a non-profit helping workers reclaim control over the data collected from them at work. We help workers turn data into power — challenging unfair algorithmic systems and holding tech platforms to account.

Who supplies, who directs, who gains

Supplies the laborgig economy workers, as the sources say, Uber drivers among them
Directs ittech platforms through opaque automated management systems (hidden algorithms) that allocate work, determine pay and assess performance
Receives the gains, bears the costsit names algorithmic wage cuts and damages drivers have suffered from Uber's dynamic pay system; the foundation states Uber's commission was set at 25% before dynamic pay, and that under dynamic pay Uber takes nearly half of the fare in some cases and keeps more than half of the fare in many cases; the Oxford and Worker Info Exchange analysis found drivers' real pay declined while Uber's profit margins rose sharply

Each as the resource itself records it. Where it records nothing, the census says so.

What this evidence can settle

Can answer

  • what data a platform holds and uses about an individual worker, as returned to a request
  • how a platform's algorithm allocated work, set pay and assessed performance for the workers whose data was obtained

Cannot answer

  • platform-wide pay or dismissal rates beyond the workers whose data is aggregated
  • the outcome of the Uber collective action (pending in the saved sources)

In the worked questions

  • What does automated management of workers look like, and can a worker see the data it uses?

Where it meets Labor Vector

rd none stated. The archive reads WIE's data access as recovering, from the worker's side, the record of how an algorithm directed the work (allocation, pay, performance), the directionality question asked of the system that directs labor.
